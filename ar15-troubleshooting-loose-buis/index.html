Troubleshooting a loose or shifting AR-15 backup sight

If groups walk or the sight feels floppy, fix the mount and detent before blaming windage dials. On Picatinny flip-ups such as Ozark Armament FBUIS-B, most “zero shift” traces to a lug not seated in a slot or a clamp that backed out.

Diagnostic tree

  1. Wiggle test. With the rifle clear, try to slide each sight along the rail. Movement means the clamp is loose or the lug missed the slot—reseat and retighten with the included hex driver.
  2. Detent test. Flip up/down. Soft lockup: clean grit from the pivot; a drop of light oil if stiff. If it will not stay up, do not trust it as a backup until resolved.
  3. Witness marks. If dials moved but clamps are tight, something bumped the adjustments—re-zero and mark again.
  4. Rail/handguard. A rotating free-float rail moves the front sight relative to the bore; torque the handguard before chasing clicks.
  5. Confirm with groups. After mechanical fixes, shoot a confirmation group before declaring the zero “back.”

Why aluminum clamps help

The listing specifies aircraft-grade aluminum construction. Rigid clamp faces resist crushing better than soft polymer when you firm up screws after a range session. Still: firm, not stripped.
